# What is ML-KEM?

### Dictionary Definition
ML-KEM (Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism) is a Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) algorithm designed using lattice-based cryptography. It performs a 'key agreement' mechanism to securely generate and share the secret keys required to encrypt data between communicating parties. As one of the post-quantum cryptography standards selected by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), it was developed to counter decryption attempts on existing asymmetric cryptographic systems (such as RSA and ECC) that leverage the powerful computational capabilities of quantum computers.

### Practical Use Case
Security infrastructure providers such as Cloudflare are applying ML-KEM to their VPN services and tunneling clients (e.g., WARP). It is utilized to protect communication data from 'Harvest-now-decrypt-later' (HNDL) attacks, where an attacker collects encrypted traffic today with the intent to decrypt it once high-performance quantum computers become available in the future.

### Related Words
- Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC): A next-generation encryption framework designed to remain secure even against the processing power of quantum computers.
- ML-DSA (Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm): A lattice-based post-quantum digital signature algorithm used for identity authentication.
- HNDL (Harvest-now-decrypt-later): A cyberattack strategy that involves collecting encrypted data now to decrypt it in the future.
