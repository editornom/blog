# What is the Bus Factor?

### Dictionary Definition
The Bus Factor is a metric used to measure the degree to which knowledge and capabilities are concentrated among a small number of individuals within a specific project or organization. It is a conceptual value that asks: "What is the minimum number of team members who must suddenly depart (e.g., get hit by a bus) for the project to come to a halt?" A lower index (e.g., 1) indicates a high level of risk due to heavy dependency on specific individuals, whereas a higher index suggests that knowledge is well-shared, leading to greater team resilience.

### Practical Use Case
When adopting technologies with steep learning curves, such as Rust, it is common to encounter situations where only a very small number of developers proficient in the language can understand and manage the code. In such instances, the Bus Factor converges toward 1, significantly increasing the risk of the entire project stalling if those developers are unavailable. Consequently, technical leaders must manage business risks by preventing knowledge silos and increasing the Bus Factor through consistent code reviews, thorough documentation, and ongoing training.
