# What is Test-time Computing?

### Dictionary Definition
Test-time Computing is a technical methodology where an AI model maximizes its performance by deploying additional computational resources during the inference stage—the point where it generates a response to a user—rather than during the training phase. This paradigm has emerged as traditional 'Scaling Laws,' which increased intelligence by expanding model size or training data volume, have begun to reach their limits in terms of capital and data efficiency. The core concept is to allow the model to explore multiple reasoning paths or undergo self-verification processes instead of producing an immediate output, thereby significantly enhancing its ability to handle complex logical reasoning and problem-solving.

### Practical Use Case
This methodology is utilized in areas requiring high levels of logical sophistication, such as proving mathematical theorems or designing complex software architectures. Instead of the model outputting an answer in a single computational pass, it employs techniques like 'Chain of Thought (CoT)' to repeat intermediate reasoning steps multiple times and self-correct errors, which ultimately increases the reliability of the final response.
