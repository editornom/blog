Simple scaling competition has hit a wall of diminishing capital efficiency, and the true path to AGI now lies in architectural innovation.</p></div>

There was an era when AI intelligence seemed to grow magically in proportion to the amount of capital invested. A 2020 paper by OpenAI researchers provided a clear roadmap for global tech companies—'bigger is better'—triggering the dawn of the Large Language Model (LLM) race.

However, signals of reaching 'The Great Plateau' are now being detected across the industry. We are witnessing the end of scaling laws as we know them, where simple economies of scale no longer guarantee exponential leaps in intelligence.

## The Myth of 'Bigger is Better': Historical Context from Kaplan to Chinchilla

### Finding the Golden Ratio of Data and Computing Resources

Prior to the <a href="/en/glossary/chinchilla-law" class="glossary-tooltip" data-definition="The principle that optimal computational efficiency is achieved when the number of model parameters and the number of training data tokens are scaled in balance.">Chinchilla Law</a> proposed by Kaplan et al. in 2020, the prevailing view was that model size (N) was the most critical variable for performance. Under the belief that capital equals intelligence, companies risked everything to increase parameter counts.

However, in 2022, DeepMind challenged this inertia. They demonstrated that true computational efficiency can only be achieved if the volume of training data is expanded at the same rate as the model size, requiring a total overhaul of AI investment strategies.

### The End of Scale-Guaranteed Intelligence

We have entered an era where building a massive neural network is no longer enough to secure a competitive edge. As shown in the table below, the scaling paradigm is rapidly shifting from quantitative expansion to qualitative efficiency.

| Law (Year) | Core Metric | Model Scaling Index (alpha) | Data Scaling Index (beta) | Key Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kaplan (2020) | Model Size (N) | 0.076 | 0.095 | Model size is the decisive factor for performance |
| Chinchilla (2022) | Compute-Optimal | 0.50 | 0.50 | Balanced expansion of model size and data |
| Quality-Aware (2025) | Data Quality (Q) | Variable | Q-based weights | Quality (Q) is more efficient than quantitative expansion |

## The Invisible Wall: The Illusion of Intelligence and the Investment Efficiency Trap

### Qualitative Stagnation and the Limits of LLMs 'Pretending to Reason'

The fluent responses of LLMs often create an illusion of intelligence. However, criticism is mounting that they are merely reproducing statistical patterns from training data rather than understanding complex logical structures.

> "The era where scale guaranteed intelligence is over. We are now in the post-scaling era, where we must prove the qualitative value and cost-effectiveness of intelligence, rather than engaging in blind parameter competition."

Scholars like Yann LeCun point out that current LLM architectures fundamentally lack a physical understanding of the world. This suggests that simply following <a href="/en/glossary/scaling-laws" class="glossary-tooltip" data-definition="The principle that AI model performance improves exponentially as model size, data volume, and computing resources increase.">scaling laws</a> will not lead to human-level AGI.

### Data Exhaustion and the Emergence of Quality Variable 'Q'

A bigger problem is the exhaustion of the data itself. High-quality text data on the internet has reached its limit, and quality—specifically data density—is emerging as the key variable for performance.

*   **Signs of a Strategic Bubble:** Despite exponential increases in GPU cluster and data center costs, the performance gains in models post-GPT-4 are starting to feel marginal.
*   **Data Quality Research (arXiv:2510.03313):** Subramanyam et al. introduced the data quality parameter 'Q,' demonstrating that if high-quality data is secured, model parameters can be drastically reduced while maintaining the same performance.
*   **The Shift to Cost-Effective Intelligence:** Models like DeepSeek R1 are countering the 'scaling is everything' narrative by achieving similar performance to Big Tech models at a fraction of the cost through efficient architectures and reasoning-based learning.

## A New Map for the Post-Scaling Era: Architectural Innovation and Efficient Intelligence

### The Rise of Test-time Compute and Reasoning Models

The industry's focus is shifting from the training process to the inference process (Test-time). Known as 'test-time computing,' this approach boosts intelligence by allowing the model to 'think' more before generating a response.

This is gaining attention as a new exit strategy to maximize reasoning capabilities without infinitely expanding expensive hardware. The remarkable achievements of OpenAI’s o1 model and DeepSeek’s R1 are products of this paradigm shift.

### AI 2027: Is the Path to AGI via Scaling or Innovation?

Optimism and skepticism collide regarding the arrival of AGI. While Sam Altman predicts 2027 and encourages investment, others warn that current architectures can never cross the 'invisible wall.'

> "The illusion of scaling created by massive capital is facing the physical limit of data exhaustion, warning of a 'strategic bubble' in the AI industry."

Ultimately, the future winners will not be those with the most servers, but those who possess the 'alchemy of algorithms' to extract intelligence most efficiently.

## Conclusion: Beyond the Strategic Bubble, Time to Consider 'Intelligence ROI'

We are standing at the first major turning point of the AI industry. The low-hanging fruit of performance gains from blind expansion is gone, and the measure of survival is now how much valuable intelligence can be produced relative to the capital invested.

The post-scaling era is not a technical stagnation but the beginning of a new innovation toward smarter, more efficient intelligence. It is time to clear the fog of the 'strategic bubble' pushed by capital and look deeper into the essence of intelligence.
